## What this session actually changed

Three bugs, **all the same shape: an identifier that names less than it needs
to.** Worth reading as one lesson, not three fixes.

1. **`SELF_CHECK_KEY`** named a priority (`liquidity`) the board had retired,
   so `wf_attach` aborted with "not in the grid".
2. **Checkpoint keys named a DATE, not a board.** Two boards were built on
   2026-09-11 — 19 strategies at 14:45, 13 at 17:50 — and the second silently
   reused all 26 of the first's partitions, finishing a ~50-minute leg in
   **0.4 min**. `if path.exists()` was the entire reuse test. Fixed with
   `wf_attach.board_key()`.
3. **`board_key` named a board, not its arithmetic.** Fixing the hold curve
   (below) left a stale `wf_attach_hold_*.pkl` the key could not reject.
   `board_key` now also hashes `wf_attach.py` and `wf_daily.py` whole.

**The `recent` hold benchmark is fixed.** `wf_daily.hold_curve` parked Rs1 in
cash from 2006 for a bucket whose 187 members do not list until 2018-01-11 —
2,984 dead sessions dragging its reported growth to 8.54%/yr against
`validation.buy_and_hold`'s 21.63%. The curve now starts the day the first
member exists: **2,141 days, 21.66%**. Other four buckets do not move.

**No statistic changed** — verified against the payload, not argued:
`recent` cells top out at **2,138 days** against 2,141 live sessions, while
other buckets reach 5,122. `excess_stats` was already intersecting the dead
span away. It was the *reported* benchmark that was wrong.

**The real defect was the guard.** `wf_daily` claimed its hold arithmetic was
asserted against `validation.buy_and_hold` but checked the `all` bucket alone —
the one bucket that structurally cannot expose a dead-span bug. It now loops
all five and raises above 1.5 pts/yr (worst gap now 0.03).

  is a defect report until proven otherwise.** Nothing errored, the checker

  edit. Verify with `python3 -c "from kitelab import signals; print(signals._SUPPORT)"`.
