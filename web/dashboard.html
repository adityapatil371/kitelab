Same rule, three timeframe stacks

Q/M/W trades weekly closes, M/W/D daily (the class strategy), W/D/H hourly. Five assigned stocks · gross · fixed ₹1L · common window per stock (hourly data exists only from 2015). Going UP a timeframe makes each trade better but rarer; going DOWN makes each trade worse but far more frequent — and charges multiply with trade count.

Your manual Bitcoin backtest — machine verdict, class rule

36 hand-logged trades checked bar-by-bar against Binance daily data, stops judged at CLOSES only. Finding 1: the sheet's Profit column ignored No. of Shares — the real result is ~48x larger. Finding 2: in 3 trades a daily close broke the stop before the claimed exit price ever traded (2 exits originally flagged were valid once judged at closes). Corrected total: ₹51,02,167 (sheet said ₹57.1L with shares fixed).

The findings that changed the numbers

Hand-picking flatters. The first manual backtests showed a 76% win rate; the machine, taking every signal, gets ~25–31%. The edge is real but lives in rare big winners, not in winning often.

Drawdown was being measured wrong. Old method sampled equity only at exits, valued open positions at cost, and divided by the wrong peak. Daily mark-to-market roughly doubled the Breakout drawdown (−30% → −59%).

The convention matters. The class checks everything at closes; a broker stop fires intrabar. Same signals, class rule: ₹1L EMA account went from 3.8% to 11.6% CAGR — shakeouts rescued — at the price of single losses up to ~2.5x the planned risk.

Rules of the game (what every number assumes)

Entry at the close that first stands 2% above all three 20-EMAs; stop = entry bar's low, checked at closes; exit on a close 2% below any EMA or at/below the stop.

Sizing: 1% of capital risked per trade, capped by cash; whole shares for stocks, fractional for assets.

Costs: exact Zerodha delivery model on stocks; flat 0.10%/0.05% per side on assets. No slippage anywhere.

Biases we can't remove: today's surviving stocks (2008 was worse in reality); Kite has no intraday before ~2015; MCX continuous futures carry rollover jumps.
